## What attribution is

Scope: the credit-assignment question. An outcome happened, a conversion, a signup, a closed deal, and multiple touches came before it. Attribution decides how the credit is split. The choice changes budgets, dashboards, and careers, which is why it is fought over.

## The models are samples, not truths

Scope: every attribution model is an illustration of one assumption about how credit should flow, not a measurement of what caused what. This page shows the classic models side by side on one worked example journey, so the reader sees the same touches credited differently:

- **Last-touch** — all credit to the final touch before the outcome. The recommended starting point for small teams (see the [conversion page](conversion.md)).
- **First-touch** — all credit to the touch that started the journey.
- **Multi-touch** — credit split across touches: linear, time-decay, position-based.
- **Data-driven** — credit assigned by a model rather than a rule.
- **Marketing mix modeling (MMM)** — top-down: regress outcomes on channel spend over time; no user-level tracking at all.
- **Incrementality** — the experimental answer: holdouts and geo tests measuring what would have happened anyway.

## Go-to-market funnel attribution

Scope: attribution inside the company, across the go-to-market funnel: marketing-sourced vs. sales-sourced pipeline, product-led vs. sales-assisted credit, which team's number a closed deal lands in.

The part most treatments skip, and the position this page takes: the attribution goal has to align with personal incentives. Attribution is not only a measurement design; it is an incentive design. A credit rule that rewards the wrong behavior gets the wrong behavior, however accurate the model.
